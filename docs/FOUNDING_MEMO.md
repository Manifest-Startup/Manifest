# Founding memo — original Manifest vision

Last reviewed **May 1, 2026**. This is an edited, grammar-cleared version of early founder notes preserved for context. For current product intent aligned with the shipped app, see [`PRODUCT_VISION.md`](./PRODUCT_VISION.md).

## Core pitch

An **ideas marketplace** is a place where people articulate new business models and ideas. Users add ideas and elaborate on them. AI helps quantify opportunities and handle supporting work—documents, patent-oriented drafts, and incorporation workflows—while founders retain granular control over what is produced.

The platform combines **discovery** with a **dashboard** for viewing, evaluating, and participating in startups, with ideas linked to intellectual property where that applies. A single user can act as **both a founder and an investor**.

The long-term vision included readiness for a **Web3**-style environment that democratizes and decentralizes value creation through startups. **Cryptocurrency integration** was envisioned as a later step; the software architecture should stay portable so that optional crypto layers can be added if the ecosystem matures—without making crypto a requirement for core value today.

## Why people sign up

The philosophy is that **many people enjoy thinking about ideas** but find **commercializing them difficult**. They come to the platform to capture ideas and see whether they gain traction.

**Would they pay directly for ideation?** Not as the default: people will not pay upfront for “yet another notes app.” The goal is to **narrow the gap between ideation and reality**. A heavy paywall would push people back to private notes and widen that gap, mirroring friction in the real world.

**Who might pay indirectly?** The platform creates value at scale for startups and founders; participation could eventually align with **subscription** (for those who prefer not to dilute equity) or a **small equity-style stake** (similar to accelerator models—for example ~1%—for those who want to share upside instead of paying upfront).

## Founder challenges (high level)

Founders face motivation gaps, bandwidth limits, fear of competition (“someone already built this”), outreach fatigue, legal and accounting overwhelm, iteration fatigue on product, and difficulties fundraising or proving traction. Investors, meanwhile, may spread bets widely without deep product usage—while customers discover strong products often through **word of mouth** rather than ads.

Manifest’s intent is to reduce busywork, surface **early similarity and literature-style research** with AI, and keep ownership, responsibility, and valuation concepts explicit as the product evolves.

## Ideal experience (directional)

Directional goals from the original notes included:

- Capture an idea once and propagate updates across **Business Model Canvas**, documents, and linked artifacts.
- **Pitch-style discovery** (short-form feed) for browsing ideas.
- Clear paths for **followers, customers, and investors**, with portfolio-oriented views where investing exists.
- World-class document formats, strong quantification of models, and **automation with manual override** throughout.

Not every bullet from early brainstorming is implemented yet; treat [`PRODUCT_VISION.md`](./PRODUCT_VISION.md) and the codebase as the truth for what ships today.

## Brand and workflow sketches

**Name — Manifest:** Something that conveys how an idea becomes a business and accelerates progress—streamlining startups, **“ideas to reality,”** and end-to-end AI-assisted structuring of business models.

**Roles:**

- **Founder:** Captures the idea; the system helps produce structured artifacts, optional diligence steps before going public, and linked research and financial inputs where product supports them.
- **Investor:** May discover ideas through marketing or feeds, review pitches and FAQs, perform diligence, and express interest through defined flows (exact mechanics evolve with product/legal).

## Principles mentioned in early notes

Examples preserved from the original memo: transparency for side-work the platform takes on; minimizing premature incorporation spend; favoring automation plus founder control; separating hype from measurable traction.
